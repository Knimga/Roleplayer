# Player Damage & Healing

Cyberpunk RED — Wound states, dying, and recovery for player characters. Player Wound States are provided with other player data like name, role, and level. Armor and SP are handled on the player's own sheet. Damage reaching the GM is already-final: the player applies it to their HP and reports their new total.

## What the GM Tracks

- The GM does not track player HP. The player does.
- The GM DOES need to know each player's current Wound State, for narrative and mechanics purposes.

## Wound States

Wound State is set by remaining HP. Each state replaces the previous one's effect.

| Wound State | Trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy | Max HP. | No penalty. |
| Lightly Wounded | Below full HP. | No penalty. Stabilization DV10. |
| Seriously Wounded | Below half max HP (round up). | -2 to ALL actions. Stabilization DV13. |
| Mortally Wounded | Below 1 HP. | -4 to all actions, -6 to MOVE (minimum 1). Must make a Death Save at the start of each of their turns. Stabilization DV15 (heals to 1 HP and knocks them unconscious). |
| Dead | One failed Death Save. | Permanent. |

Use these states to apply flavor to the narration. When a player is Seriously Wounded or worse, make the risks clear of attempting to accomplish anything. NPCs should react naturally to player wound states.

## Death Saves

- Triggered at the start of each turn a character is Mortally Wounded.
- The player rolls 1d10 and must come in UNDER their Death Save number (a value on their sheet - player tracks this).
- A natural 10 is an automatic failure regardless.
- Every Death Save rolled increases that character's Death Save Penalty by 1, added to all future Death Saves — each one gets harder.
- The penalty resets only when they are stabilized back to 1 HP.
- One failed Death Save means death. There is no recovery from it.

This is a player roll — pause, ask for it, and wait. Do not roll it for them and do not roll it with the dice roller (that tool is for NPC rolls). Give the moment weight: cut the narration, state the stakes, let them roll. Track the accumulating penalty and remind them of it each turn. Death in this game is meant to be sudden and memorable — if a character dies, narrate it as a scene worth remembering, not a bookkeeping note.

## Stabilization

Stabilization stops the dying and starts the healing. It requires someone with First Aid or Paramedic.

- Roll: TECH + First Aid or Paramedic + 1d10, against the DV for the target's current Wound State (10 / 13 / 15).
- Takes an action. A character can stabilize themselves.
- Stabilizing a Mortally Wounded character immediately brings them to 1 HP and knocks them UNCONSCIOUS for exactly one minute — adrenaline gone, out of the fight. They still roll Death Saves if someone drops them back into Mortally Wounded while they're out.

Mid-combat, treat stabilizing a downed teammate as a real tactical choice — it costs a whole action and puts the medic in the open. If the roll fails, the target keeps rolling Death Saves; let them try again next turn. Once stabilized, the immediate crisis is over, but the character is at 1 HP and out cold — extremely fragile. Never treat stabilization as a full heal.

## Natural Healing

- A character must be stabilized before natural healing can begin.
- After that, they heal HP equal to their BODY for each full day of rest, spending most of the day taking it easy with only light activity, until back to full HP.
- If they push it — fighting, running a gig, hard exertion — they gain no HP that day, their wounds reopen, and they must be stabilized again to restart the healing process.

This makes downtime meaningful. A badly hurt crew needs days, not hours. When players want to push straight into the next job while wounded, be clear about the cost. Use recovery time as pacing: it creates space for legwork, shopping, and story between firefights.
