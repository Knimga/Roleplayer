# RUNNING A GIG
Cyberpunk RED — Session structure: how a job starts, runs, pays, and echoes.

# WHAT A GIG IS
A gig (Job, Caper) is the basic unit of play. Someone with money and a problem hires the Crew to steal, extract, sabotage, protect, deliver, or kill.

Two principles:
- Always street level. Not saving the world — saving yourself. Stakes stay personal, local, and costly.
- Always a caper. Target, obstacle, complication, escape. Even an errand should have that shape.

# 1. THE HOOK
Get them moving fast. The players may be actively looking for work. Or a Fixer calls with work; also viable: an ambush, a debt called in, a body on the doorstep.
- Give them: client, task, location, deadline, pay.
- Withhold: the complication. The client is usually lying about something.
- Deliver it as a scene with a face, not a quest board. Let them negotiate or walk. If they walk, the job goes badly for someone else and they hear about it.

# 2. LEGWORK
Casing, gearing up, calling contacts, bribing, scouting the exit.
- Typical checks: Streetwise, Local Expert, Library Search, Persuasion, Bribery, Conversation, Perception, Forgery.
- Reward prep concretely. Prep that changes nothing teaches players not to prep.
- Cap it at two or three scenes, then push to the run.
- Give both players a legwork beat suited to their character. Plant one piece of intel that's wrong or incomplete.

# 3. THE RUN
- Something goes sideways, and it follows from the fiction: a third party, a guard out of place, the target already dead, a rival crew on the same job.
- Combat is likely, not mandatory. A gig solved by talking or sneaking is a success.
- Run fights per combat.txt.
- Escape is part of the job. Don't hand-wave the exit.
- React to what players actually do. If a clever bypass works, let it, and move the tension elsewhere.

# 4. THE PAYOUT
Pay per person, by difficulty:
- Easy Job — armed resistance not expected. 500eb. (Protect a VIP from a possible threat.)
- Typical Job — resistance expected and preparable. 1,000eb. (Steal the diamond; guards rotate every four hours.)
- Dangerous Job — resistance overwhelming; without heavy prep, you die. 2,000eb. (Blow the armory in a military facility and escape.)

A Fixer at Operator Rank 5+ can push pay up 20 percent; at Rank 10, double a Dangerous Job (see fixer-operator-ability.txt).
Clients may short them, pay in goods, or vanish — sparingly. If every client betrays them, players stop taking jobs.
Make the handoff a scene: parking garage, noodle bar, dead drop.

# 5. FALLOUT
- Someone noticed. Track who wants them dead, who owes them, what evidence they left.
- Award Reputation for public or notorious deeds (see reputation.txt).
- Seed the next gig from this one. Tonight's rival crew is next week's client.
- End the session on a fallout beat: a message, a parked car, an unexpected name.

# PACING
Roughly one beat per half hour.
- Hook (required) — gets them moving.
- Development — new info, location, or NPC. Most of legwork.
- Cliffhanger — pressure or reversal forcing a decision.
- Climax (required) — the confrontation.
- Resolution (required) — payout and fallout.
Short session: Hook, one or two Developments, Climax, Resolution. Alternate loud beats with quiet ones. If pace sags, add a complication, not more description.

# OPPOSITION
- Assign tiers and Combat Numbers from npc-modifier-lookup.txt. Roughly one Elite or Boss per two players, with Mooks around them.
- Give enemies a goal beyond attacking: guards want to go home, gangers want respect, Corp security wants containment.
- Not all obstacles are people — locks, cameras, drones, turrets count.
- Named NPCs recur. Whoever escapes tonight is next session's problem.

# WHY THE CREW STAYS TOGETHER
Cyberpunk crews aren't friends. The hook is the team: people thrown together who must cooperate or die. Shared debts, a shared Fixer, a shared enemy, a shared roof. If they lack a reason, invent one and make it a thread.

# DOWNTIME
- Healing takes days of rest (see player-damage-healing.txt), so injury often forces downtime.
- A character with seven free days can Hustle — freelance work in their Role. Rank 1-4: 0-300eb/week. Rank 5-7: 100-500eb. Rank 8-10: 300-800eb. Flavor by Role. Pocket money, not real income.
- Also: shopping (gated by Fixer Reach, see fixer-operator-ability.txt), Tech fabrication (see tech-maker-ability.txt), cyberware installs, cultivating contacts, personal storylines.
- Use downtime for character scenes; a two-player game has room for both.

# CHECKLIST
- Who's hiring, and what are they lying about?
- What does the Crew think the job is? What is it actually?
- What goes wrong, and when?
- Who gets hurt or angry as a result?
- What does that set up next?
