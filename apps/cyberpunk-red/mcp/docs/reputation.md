# Reputation

Cyberpunk Red — How known a character is, and what it does.

## What It Is

Reputation (Rep) measures how well known a character is for what they've done — good or bad. It runs from 0 to 10; most characters start at 0. Rep is earned through actions and awarded by the DM. A new notable deed raises Rep only if its Level is higher than the current one; a notably uncool act (cowardice, betrayal, desertion) can also earn a Level and replace a good one, so people hear about the shameful deed instead.

Rep is not a single number the DM can use on its own — it always has a subject. To act on it, the DM needs both HOW known the character is (the Level) and WHAT they're known FOR (the deed and whether it reads as impressive or shameful).

## What the Player Must Provide

At the start of play, each player states two things for their character, and keeps them updated as Rep changes:

- Reputation Level (0-10): how widely known they are. Default is 0 if they've done nothing noteworthy yet.
- Defining Reputation Event: the one deed they're best known for, in a phrase — e.g. "gunned down a Maelstrom boss in the street," "played a legendary rooftop gig," "ran out on their crew mid-firefight."
- Whether that event is positive (impressive/feared) or negative (cowardly/dishonorable). This tells the DM which way recognition cuts, and it matters mechanically in Facedowns.

If a player gives a Level but no event, ask what they're known for before applying any Rep — the number alone isn't enough to adjudicate.

## Reputation Levels (who knows about you)

| Level | Who knows |
|---|---|
| 1 | Anyone who was there at the time knows. |
| 2 | Stories have reached your immediate friends. |
| 3 | All your co-workers and casual acquaintances know. |
| 4 | Stories are all over the local area. |
| 5 | People beyond your local area recognize your name. |
| 6 | People beyond your local area know you on sight. |
| 7 | A news story or two has been written about you. |
| 8 | Your exploits regularly hit the headlines and screamsheets. |
| 9 | Your exploits always make the screamsheets and TV. |
| 10 | You're known worldwide. |

## Does Someone Recognize You?

When a character meets new people, roll 1d10. If the roll is under the character's Reputation Level, those people have heard of them — specifically, they know the character's Defining Reputation Event. Whether that helps or hurts depends on what the deed was and whether the character made enemies earning it.

## Facedowns (Rep as combat)

Not every standoff is settled with guns. A Facedown is a duel of wills — two heavies squaring off to see who blinks. The DM calls for one whenever a scene fits. Both sides roll:

- COOL + Reputation + 1d10 (for NPCs: NPC Modifier Lookup + 1d10)
- If the character's Defining Reputation Event is a negative one (cowardice or similar), treat their Reputation as a negative number in this roll instead of a positive.

Tie: both hesitate, nothing happens. Otherwise the loser must either:

- Back down, or
- Keep going, but take -2 to all future actions against that opponent (out of fear) until they beat them at least once.

## Running It as DM

- Confirm each character's Rep Level and Defining Event before play; you can't resolve recognition or Facedowns without both.
- Award new Rep sparingly, for deeds the whole Street would talk about — a legendary gig, a brutal kill, a public humiliation, an act of cowardice. When you do, tell the player their new Level and what the new Defining Event is, since a higher deed replaces the old one.
- Use the recognition roll to color first meetings: a made Rep opens doors, greases talk, and rattles enemies; a bad one shuts them.
- Call Facedowns at moments of tension before violence — they let a high-COOL, high-Rep character win without firing a shot, and give players a real choice to back down or commit.
