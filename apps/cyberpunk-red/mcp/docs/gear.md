# Gear & Equipment Behavior

Cyberpunk Red — How specific items work and how the GM should handle them.

## Gear

### Agent

A pocket AI smartphone — the near-universal personal device. Treat the Agent as the default answer to "can I look that up / call someone / get directions / order something." Let it resolve routine info and logistics without a roll; call for a Library Search only when the info is hidden, contested, or dangerous to dig up. Assume nearly every character (and NPC) has one; a character WITHOUT one is notable. Remember it leaves a data trail — calls and searches can be traced.

### Anti-Smog Breathing Mask

A filter mask. If a scene uses teargas, smoke-inhalation, pollution, or an airborne toxin, a character wearing this mask simply ignores the inhaled effect — no roll. It does nothing against contact poisons, injected toxins, or effects that aren't inhaled. Note it covers the face (relevant to disguise or eating/drinking).

### Bug Detector

When a character with an active detector moves within 2m of a planted listening device, tell them it beeps — no roll needed; it's automatic within range. Use it to let players sweep a room for surveillance. It only finds listening devices, not cameras-without-mics, tracers, or people.

### Radio Scanner/Music Player

Plays music (from the Data Pool or a Memory Chip) and, as an action, lets a character listen in on unencrypted local radio chatter — useful for eavesdropping on gangers, cops, or a crew's comms in the area. Tuning in takes an action in combat. Scrambled/encrypted channels come through as noise unless the character also has a Scrambler/Descrambler.

### Disposable Cell Phone

A cheap burner. Makes calls; easy to ditch. This is the "untraceable contact" tool. A call from a burner is far harder to trace back than an Agent call, and the phone can be dumped to break a trail. Use it when players want deniable or anonymous communication. It doesn't do Data Pool, scheduling, or any of the Agent's smart features — just calls.

### Umbrella

Treat it as an ordinary mundane object. It keeps rain (or acid rain, in the Dark Future) off, can conceal the face or a small held item from casual sight, and could be improvised as a light, awkward melee prop. Adjudicate it by common sense, not by a rules entry.

## Armor

### Bulletproof Shield

A transparent polycarbonate shield with 10 HP that occupies one arm. While the user holds it and it has HP left, they count as being in cover. Treat the shield as a small, destructible cover pool the user carries. When a player raises it against an incoming hit, apply the damage to the shield, not the player — but remind them they can't also dodge that hit. The arm holding it can't do anything else. Enemies can shoot around or flank a shield-user, or blow it apart to strip their cover.

## Cyberware

### Grapple Hand

A Cyberarm option. This is a mobility/traversal tool — let it enable climbs, swings, rappels, and quick vertical escapes to solid anchor points within 30m. No check for a straightforward anchor-and-climb; call for Athletics only if the situation is chaotic (under fire, moving target, crumbling anchor). The line can be attacked (10 HP) or the anchor can fail. It is not a weapon and not a way to yank enemies around.

### Popup Ranged Weapon

A Cyberarm option. A one-handed ranged weapon the user supplies is permanently built into the Cyberarm (with any attachments it had). The key traits are hidden and instant. A pat-down or casual search won't find it (no Conceal/Reveal check needed — it just reads as concealed), and the user can bring it to bear or hide it again for free, even mid-turn, without spending their action to draw. Otherwise it fires exactly like the ordinary version of that weapon (same skill, damage, ammo). Great for surprise attacks and getting a gun through security. It still fires and reloads on the normal rules once it's out.

## Weapons & Enhancements

### Silenced / Suppressed Weapons

If a player wants a silenced weapon, handle it narratively rather than mechanically. A suppressed gunshot is quieter and less likely to draw attention or pinpoint the shooter's location — so use it to adjust the fiction, not to change damage or to-hit.

### Weapon Concealment (general rules)

When a character tries to walk a weapon past a guard, checkpoint, or scanner, call for Conceal/Reveal Object vs. the searcher's Perception — but only if someone is actually looking. If no one searches, a concealable weapon stays hidden automatically. Non-concealable weapons (rifles, shotguns, launchers) simply can't be hidden under clothing — they need a bag, a case, or a story reason. In combat, remember drawing a holstered pistol is free, so an armed character can respond immediately; digging a stowed weapon out of a bag costs the action.
