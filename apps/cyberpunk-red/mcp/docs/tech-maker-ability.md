# TECH ROLE ABILITY: MAKER
# What the GM needs to know to adjudicate a Tech at the table.

# WHAT IT IS
The Tech is the builder and fixer of the Dark Future — the one who can repair, improve, modify, fabricate, and invent gear in a world whose supply chains the War shattered. Their Role Ability is Maker, rated by a Maker Rank from 1 to 10. Maker is not a single power but a set of four Specialties. Each time a Tech raises their Maker Rank by 1, they put 1 Rank into TWO different Specialties of their choice, so a given Tech is strong in some areas and weak in others — always ask which Specialties (and Ranks) a Tech has before adjudicating.

# THE FOUR SPECIALTIES
- Field Expertise: On-the-spot repair mastery. Adds its Rank to the Tech's Basic Tech, Cybertech, Electronics/Security Tech, Weaponstech, and Land/Sea/Air Vehicle Tech checks made for ordinary purposes. Also lets the Tech jury-rig a broken item back to perfect condition as a single action (rather than a long repair); the fix holds 10 minutes per Rank, then the item reverts and can't be jury-rigged again until fully repaired.
- Upgrade Expertise: Permanently improves an existing item in one specific way (an item can carry only one such upgrade). Examples: bump an Average-quality weapon to Excellent, add a slot for options/attachments, make a one-handed weapon concealable, add +1 SP to armor that already has SP, reduce a piece of cyberware's Humanity Loss, or install one of the Tech's own inventions.
- Fabrication Expertise: Builds an existing item (or one the Tech has invented) from raw materials.
- Invention Expertise: Designs an entirely new item or a new upgrade from scratch (see below — this is the one to watch).

# THE MAKER ROLL (repair / upgrade / fabricate)
To upgrade or fabricate, the Tech rolls:
- TECH + the TECH Skill normally used to repair that kind of item + their Rank in the relevant Specialty + 1d10
The DV and the time come from the item's price category (see the ladder). On a failure, the Tech discovers halfway through that they must start over; the materials and the item survive.
Materials cost:
- Upgrading: buy materials equal to the item's own price category (vehicle upgrades count as Very Expensive/1,000eb).
- Fabricating: buy materials one price category lower than the item (Super Luxury items cost half their price in materials).
(This is the same DV/time ladder used for ordinary repairs — see the skills doc.)

# UPGRADE / FABRICATE / INVENT — DV & TIME
- Cheap/Everyday: DV9, 1 hour
- Costly: DV13, 6 hours
- Premium: DV17, 1 day
- Expensive: DV21, 1 week
- Very Expensive: DV24, 2 weeks
- Luxury: DV29, 1 month
- Super Luxury: DV29, 1 month per 10,000eb of cost

# INVENTION EXPERTISE (handle with care)
This is the Specialty most likely to break a game, so the GM drives it:
- The player describes the desired item or upgrade as precisely as they can, in terms of existing in-setting technology, including the mechanism by which it works. A rough schematic helps.
- If the GM approves the concept, the GM writes how it works in rules terms, keeping it balanced. The existing Upgrade Expertise options are the benchmark for a fair "power level."
- The GM sets the invention's price category by what it would sell for, but never lower than Expensive. That category then sets the DV, time, and material cost to actually build it (via Fabrication or Upgrade Expertise).
- Nobody takes an invention seriously until there's a working prototype — and that's exactly when rivals try to steal it. The courts are no help.
- The GM may retune or even replace an invention after the fact if it proves unbalanced; this is expected, not a failure.

# RUNNING IT AS GM
- Ask first what Maker Specialties the Tech has and at what Rank. A Tech with Field Expertise 4 is a superb field mechanic but may have no ability to invent; treat each Specialty separately.
- Field Expertise is the in-mission clutch: use it when gear fails mid-job. Remember the jury-rig is temporary — it buys minutes, not a permanent fix, and sets up tension if the fight runs long.
- Upgrade and Fabrication are usually downtime activities: set the DV and time from the price category, confirm the Tech bought materials, and let the roll decide. Don't call for these mid-firefight.
- For Invention, slow down and collaborate. Approve the concept, assign a price category (Expensive or higher), and only then let them build it. Keep the Upgrade Expertise list beside you as your balance yardstick, and don't be afraid to adjust later.
- Materials cost real eb — a Tech isn't making things for free. Track that the materials were purchased and are consumed on use.
