# Backstory: Vidik

Vidik grew up in Santo Domingo, an overpacked borough in Night City’s southeast where poverty, gang activity, and vast industrial sprawls create a people who are hard-living, hard-working, and hard-playing. Dingy allies wind between stacked slums and mechshops, workers pack the dive bars after finishing double-shifts at the towering, soot-belching factories of the Heywood Industrial Zone, and the rot of gang activity clashes with the indomitable warmth of common folk who rely on each other and persevere.

To be a child on the streets of Santo Domingo is to flirt with a future life of crime, but with the nearby industrial areas creating dozens of machineshops, techsmiths and fabricators, Vidik’s curious mind was quickly redirected. He became apprenticed to an old mechanic who ran a cramped shop fixing construction rigs, especially Zhirafa tech which was all-too-common in the area. The old mechanic paid “protection” to a local gang named the Ashliners, and in his late teens Vidik got pulled into paying that debt off directly — running jobs for the gang using his growing skills: rigging vehicles, jury-rigging weapons, sabotaging rival equipment. At first it was occasional and somewhat low-risk, but as he got older it escalated.

One job went bad. He and his team were ambushed, a piece of heavy machinery he had been rigging malfunctioned, and he lost his left arm. His team was able to retreat and get him to safety, but he remained in critical condition. The Ashliners had taken a liking to him, and in order to pay for a new arm, sent thugs to the old mechanic’s shop, ransacked it for sellable parts, and ended up killing the mechanic in the process. Vidik awoke with a cyberarm. Gang leaders explained what they had done to mitigate the cost of it, but told Vidik the debt was not fully paid, wanting to keep the useful Tech in their employ. Vidik nursed his mourning and rage internally in an effort to stay on the good side of the Ashliners, and over the next few years worked off the rest of his debt doing dangerous jobs nobody else wanted until he finally bought himself out and left.

He has since become a mercenary, drifting from job to job, seeing the wider city, proving his usefulness time and time again until his name came up on enough rosters to catch the eye of a Fixer by the name of Desmond Lorentz (“Null”). The two have recently become unlikely partners.

Vidik has emerged a hardened, resourceful techsmith, taking his knack for fighting from his gang days, his love of metal and gears from the mechanic’s havens of Santo Domingo, and his warm, indomitable spirit from the working people there. He loves a good fight, a good drink, and a good smoke. He’s quick to laugh, but his jovial boisterousness quickly turns to quiet, contented concentration when given a piece of tech that needs tweaking. Having escaped the vicious poverty-loop of Santo Domingo, Vidik is content now to make money and have fun doing it.
