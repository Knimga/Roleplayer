## Dark Magic

- An umbrella term, used mainly by the [[Southern Kingdoms]], for the schools of magic considered unacceptable by southern cultural and legal standards. There is no corresponding "light magic" category — dark magic is defined by what it includes, not by opposition to a counterpart.
- **Schools covered by the term:** necromancy, demonology, planar magic, shadow/void magic, curses and hexes, and other traditions the south finds unpalatable.
- **Origin:** First discovered and studied in [[Ythria]] during the Arcane Renaissance, initially as a scholarly pursuit rather than a moral category.
- **Southern view:** Dangerous, correctly restricted by the [[Arcane Laws]], and closely associated with the disaster that destroyed Ythria and caused the [[Demonic War]].
- **Northern/northmen view:** Broadly accepted, in some cases openly revered — see Northmen entry (Factions doc). Common everyday uses include necromancy for sleepless watchmen or testimony from the dead, protective curses, predator-deterring mind-control magic, bound demons for labor, and combat-oriented shadow magic.
- **Institutional stance:** The [[Keepers]] exist specifically to hunt down dark magic practitioners and enforce the [[Arcane Laws]] against them.
- **State practice:** [[Uldurok]] has built its entire legal and social order around mastering dark magic openly, in explicit contrast to Ythria's more secretive, ultimately catastrophic pursuit of the same knowledge.

## The Arcane Laws

- Legal code originating in [[Kirinar]], later spreading through the rest of the [[Southern Kingdoms]]. Governs which forms of magic are permitted.
- Arose as a reaction to the unrestrained magical experimentation of the Arcane Renaissance and the catastrophe at [[Ythria]].
- **Currently prohibited under the Arcane Laws:**
  - Raising of the undead (resurrection of the recently dead remains permitted)
  - Overriding of free will (mere influence or suggestion remains permitted)
  - Planar magic — the opening of portals to other planes
  - Demonic magic — summoning or commanding demons
  - Shadow/void magic - potent offensive spells using dark energies
  - Curses/hexes
- **Enforcement:** Carried out primarily by the [[Keepers]], an order with heavy influence in Kirinar's government, formed specifically to police these laws.
- **Regional variance:** Strictly and often broadly interpreted in the south (some communities treat entire schools like necromancy or enchantment with suspicion regardless of specific effect); enforcement and cultural weight decrease moving north, where the laws are largely ignored.
- **Northern reception:** Regarded by critics, historically including [[Ythria]] itself, as an expression of southern political domination rather than genuine safety concern.

## Black Tomes

- Rare, dangerous, ancient books originally written in [[Ythria]], containing the most powerful and dangerous dark magic spells and rituals known.
- Highly illegal under southern law. The [[Keepers]] actively seek to locate and destroy every remaining copy.
- Knowledge from these tomes is understood to have spread beyond Ythria via fleeing scholars and renegade mages, particularly during the [[Great Exodus]].
- At least one confirmed tome originated from the [[Clanwoods]] — the specific source of the ritual King Tyrik of [[Esterlin]] used to transform himself into a lich, precipitating the [[War of the Dead]].
- The south's fear of further tomes still circulating in the north is the direct justification for the Keepers' ongoing northern campaign (see History doc, 492AK).

## Demonic War Relics

- Weapons, armor, and other grim instruments carried by demonic commanders during the [[Demonic War]], left scattered across the continent when their bearers fell.
- Forged in the Abyss; do not become inert with their original bearers' deaths.
- No central recovery or full accounting was ever completed. [[Kirinar]]'s magisters launched recovery efforts at the time, but the war's reach far exceeded the south's ability to track every relic — many were found instead by hunters, exiles, and warbands, especially in the wilder north where southern authority carries little weight.
- **Uldurok's relationship to these relics:** Migrant bands entering the Bitter Heights during the Great Exodus carried some relics with them as trophies or curiosities. Uldurok's sorcerers recognized their true value and began serious, sustained study — the relics are now stored in the Black Fortress's deepest vaults alongside the kingdom's own arsenal, and their mastery has become central to Uldurok's ambitions.
- **Unresolved thread:** Many relics remain unaccounted for in the wild, especially in the north. Their number, location, and current possessors are not fully known to any single power in Laria.

## Wyrdkin

- The leadership convention among [[Northern Clans]] tribes: each clan or tribe is led by its own Wyrdkin, a small group of shamans, witches, seers, or dark sorcerers.
- Serve a dual role as both spiritual leaders and loremasters of their tribe.
- **Origin:** The convention dates back to the [[Great Exodus]], when learned Ythrian mages fleeing south often became the de facto leaders of the migrant caravans moving north.
- Wyrdkin leadership is a major reason northmen societies broadly accept dark magic practice — dark magic and tribal leadership have been intertwined since the north's earliest settlement.
