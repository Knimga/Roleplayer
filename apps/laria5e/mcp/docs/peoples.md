## Elves

- **Homeland:** [[Tieryf Vale]], full and unbroken extent — stretches from the Southlands to the eastern coast, including [[Nahrûn]], a living, forested elven settlement.
- **Lifespan:** Exceptionally long. Basis for elven wisdom carrying unusual political weight in Larian affairs.
- **Governance:** Loose circles of elders. Deliberation is slow; decisions, once made, are rarely revisited.
- **Default posture:** Aloof, non-interventionist. Prefer not to involve themselves in others' conflicts. Watching the current war, not mobilized.
- **Historical exception to that posture:** the [[Demonic War]] — came out of Tieryf Vale in force to help defeat the demon incursion. The only large-scale intervention of its kind on record.
- **Ythria exception:** Elven scholars in [[Ythria]] were among the most ambitious practitioners of dark magic during the Arcane Renaissance. After Ythria fell, some elven survivors did not return to Tieryf Vale — went north, and generations later became the teachers who trained Uldurok's first sorcerers. Most Tieryf Vale elves avoid discussing this.
- **Amaron Vale connection:** Elves lightly explored/settled the region during the Elder Era, before the [[Great Exodus]] migrations arrived. Left behind: place names (Amrûn → Amaron), positive relations with later settlers, and elven citizens/leaders in the founding of the [[Eastern Kingdoms]].
- **Dragonborn connection:** Dragons choose only elves as partners for producing dragonborn offspring (see Dragonborn, below).

## Dwarves

- **Origin:** Took to the mountains, [[Nahlia Mountains]] first. Founded [[Kaggrim]] at a natural spring, valley's end.
- **Kaggrim:** Oldest continuously-inhabited city in Laria; standing, ancient, populous.
- **Core value:** Build for centuries, not decades. A project's worth is measured by whether descendants generations removed still rely on it.
- **Three distinct dwarven traditions** (share root culture + respect for craft, little else — friction/rivalry between them is a live topic):
  1. **Kaggrim / [[Nahlia Mountains]]** — the original tradition.
  2. **[[Amaron Vale]]** — descendants of dwarves who fled the Nahlia Mountains eastward during the [[Great Exodus]] after the range was overrun by demons post-Ythria. Alongside gnomes. Built early infrastructure of [[Esterlin]] and [[Korsa]].
  3. **[[Taigaal]] (Zuriel Peaks)** — a tiny, obscure mining village, unknown outside its own region; no non-dwarven residents on record. Its geomancer tradition exists but is untested and unrecognized by the wider dwarven world or by outsiders generally.

## Humans

- **Defining trait:** None — the only people found in every region of Laria, shaped by local surroundings rather than shared culture.
- **Regional human cultures:**
  - **[[Southern Kingdoms]] / [[Kirinar]]:** Pragmatic builders of law and order; not always just.
  - **[[Midlands]]:** Proud, independent, strong warrior tradition. Descendants of [[Rhodan]]'s kingdom, destroyed in the [[Harrowing of the Midlands]].
  - **[[Northern Clans]] / [[Uldurok]]:** Built substantially on human ambition and human sorcery.
  - **[[Esterlin]] / [[Korsa]] ([[Eastern Kingdoms]]):** Southern-descended via the [[Great Exodus]], but hold southern zealotry (re: Arcane Laws) only loosely.
- **Historical pattern:** Most volatile people's history of any group. Elder Era Southlands saw dozens of kingdoms rise and fall before Kirinar endured. Humans wrote the [[Arcane Laws]] and broke them; human ambition raised Uldurok from conquered rubble.

## Orcs

- **Homeland:** None fixed — frontier people. Forest edges, high unclaimed mountain reaches, contested borderlands.
- **Social unit:** Clan, not crown. Small, fiercely loyal bands, each shaped by local terrain more than by any unified orcish tradition.
- **Religion:** Worship of [[Bregga]] ("the Forsaken Queen") — goddess of hardship/endurance tied to the [[Dark Era]]. Not orc-exclusive: also venerated by northmen. One of the few shared faiths across otherwise-distrustful peoples.
- **Relations with neighbors:** Highly variable by location — ranges from uneasy tolerance to fragile, tense coexistence at contested borderlands, depending on local history and need.

## Gnomes

- **Origin:** Mountain people, historically linked to dwarves. Fled the [[Nahlia Mountains]] eastward during the [[Great Exodus]] alongside dwarves, after the range was overrun by demons.
- **Core value:** Precision and cleverness (elegance of solution) — contrast to dwarven "built to last centuries" ethos.
- **Notable work:** Early infrastructure of the [[Eastern Kingdoms]] (Esterlin, Korsa) during their founding.
- **Temperament:** Anxious, detail-obsessed. Talented and essential in brute-confidence-driven cultures, though such placements can leave them perpetually a little flustered. Some carry long-term private doubt about whether their work will last as intended.

## Kitsune (Foxfolk)

- **Rarest, least understood people in Laria.**
- **Homeland:** [[Westwood]], old-growth reaches specifically. Kin in spirit to the druidic enclaves there since before written record.
- **Magic:** Nature/fate-oriented rather than arcane. Oracular sight appears among them more often than chance should allow.
- **Defining trait — hidden true form:** Carry a truer shape beneath the one shown to outsiders; keep it hidden by choice, not necessity. A traveling Foxfolk usually passes as an ordinary outsider; many live entire lives without anyone outside their own kind learning the difference.
- **Current stance:** Uninvolved in the southern/northern war, same as the rest of the Westwood.
- **DM note:** Numbers, true form, and society structure are deliberately unspecified in the source lore. Treat as an intentional gap, not an oversight.

## Halflings

- **Homeland:** None fixed, like humans — but ambition runs toward trade/commerce, not conquest or crowns.
- **Roles:** Caravan masters, financiers, brewers, merchants.
- **Core value:** Reliability over reputation. A halfling who delivers on a deal is worth more than one who wins an argument about honor.

---

## Dragonborn

- **Not a lineage that breeds true** — each is individually born of a true dragon and a biped (almost exclusively elves; dragons approach no one else directly).
- **Rarity:** Uncommon. Occurs "now and again," not on any regular basis.
- **Upbringing:** Always claimed at birth by the dragon parent. Raised in hidden northern enclaves ([[Bitter Heights]]) among dragon-adopted [[Kobolds]]. Reasoning given: no biped society is trusted to raise draconic blood without exploiting or endangering it.
- **Cultural imprint:** Patience over haste, knowledge hoarded not shared, reverence for lineage taught early enough to become near-doctrine.
- **Public behavior:** Unlike Foxfolk or Warborn, dragonborn do not hide their heritage — taught to carry it with pride.
- **Narrative flag:** A dragonborn encountered outside the northern enclaves is not "just wandering" — their presence implies something significant drew them out.

## Warborn

Umbrella term (imposed by scholars, not a name most Warborn use themselves) for several bloodlines with no traceable Larian origin, first documented in the long aftermath of the [[Demonic War]].

- **Scholarly consensus (uneasy, not fully settled):** Bloodlines trace to the chaos/violence the demonic horde left behind, most concentrated in the war-torn north.
- **Not demonic in nature.** Face superstition and suspicion regardless of individual conduct — a tiefling's horns or a shifter's sudden bestial trait is enough to draw suspicion from some.
- **Distribution:** Bloodlines can lie dormant for generations before surfacing unannounced in an ordinary family. Most communities have only a handful; more often solitary or small clusters than dedicated Warborn communities.

**Subtypes:**
- **Tieflings** — most numerous (still rare overall). Infernal features (horns, tails, off-color eyes) make them the most visible target of Warborn-directed suspicion.
- **Minotaurs** — solitude or small family clusters in remote, defensible terrain (mountain holds, ruins). Disinterest in smaller peoples' affairs, not hostility.
- **Kenku** — least understood even by scholars who study the others. Nomadic/fringe. Natural mimicry makes them effective scouts, messengers, spies.
- **Lizardfolk** — river deltas, coastal marsh, wetland. Pragmatic, clannish, largely outside kingdom politics.
- **Centaurs** — open plains, wild borderland. Independence comparable to Midlanders; many clusters found in or near the [[Midlands]] specifically.
- **Shifters** — pass most easily among other peoples; bestial traits often dormant/subtle. Some never learn their own heritage.

## Related Non-Player Peoples

### Dragons
- **Population:** Very few remain — a handful of ancient survivors, reduced from a larger population by the [[Demonic War]].
- **Society:** Entirely separate from the rest of Laria — unwritten, unmapped, essentially unknown even to nearby peoples.
- **Elder Era contact:** Only voluntary contact was with [[Elves]] — rare, in elf-shape, unexplained, faded out of living memory. Accidental sightings were mostly witnessed by orc clans, dwarven prospectors, gnomish tinkerers, and rare northern humans.
- **Demonic War:** Specifically hunted by demons in a deliberate, largely unrecorded campaign — almost no one alive at the time knew it happened.
- **Current status:** The last survivors live in the deepest [[Bitter Heights]] cave systems among [[Kobolds]] (not ruling them — kobolds treat dragons as elder kin/living gods by their own choice). Occasionally still produce dragonborn offspring with elves.

### Kobolds
- **Homeland:** [[Bitter Heights]] cave systems.
- **Origin:** Small, reptilian, struggling population, later adopted by the last surviving dragons as informal wards/kin, not subjects. Kobolds treat dragons as elder kin or living gods.
- **Current status:** Scattered enclaves throughout the northern caves, fiercely protective of secrets they "only half-understand."
